This is just the set of prompts on which the system is being tested on 

These prompts are made difficult on purpose to check if the system can handle complex and challenging senarios
They dont have actual answers for these reasons and span several fields



PROMPT 1:
Design a turbine blade for a jet engine operating at sustained gas-path temperatures above 1400°C,
withstanding centrifugal and thermal stress at operating RPM. Specify and justify a material class 
(nickel superalloy vs. ceramic matrix composite) against the thermal load. Design an internal cooling 
scheme (film or convective) since the base alloy cannot survive 1400°C alone. Specify a thermal barrier
coating strategy. Estimate fatigue life under cyclic takeoff-cruise-landing thermal loading. Blade
must fit standard axial-flow turbine geometry, minimize weight without compromising fatigue life, 
and remain manufacturable via investment casting or additive manufacturing. Show reasoning and 
calculations, not just a final answer.



PROMPT 2:
Design a low-power analog front-end for a wearable single-lead ECG monitor targeting a 6-month battery
life on a 100mAh cell. Specify ADC resolution and sampling rate sufficient to resolve QRS complexes
without aliasing. Design an input-referred noise budget below 5µVrms including instrumentation
amplifier and filter stage choices. Specify a bandpass filter strategy that rejects baseline wander
and 50/60Hz mains interference without distorting the QRS waveform. Justify a power budget breakdown 
across analog and digital blocks against the battery life target. Address motion artifact tolerance 
for a chest-strap form factor. Recommend a process node and package suitable for a coin-cell-class 
wearable. Show reasoning and calculations, not just a final part selection.


PROMPT 3:
Design a distributed rate limiter for a multi-region API gateway handling 500K requests/sec with
per-tenant quotas. Choose and justify a consistency model (strict vs. eventual) for shared quota
state across regions, given cross-region latency of 50-150ms. Specify a sharding/partitioning strategy
for quota counters that avoids hot-key contention. Design a failure-handling strategy for a region 
losing connectivity to the quota store, including fallback behavior and reconciliation on recovery.
Bound the worst-case over-admission a tenant could achieve during a network partition. Specify a
data structure and eviction strategy for the counters that fits a sub-millisecond per-request 
latency budget. Show reasoning and complexity/latency estimates, not just a final architecture name.


PROMPT 4:
Analyze the convergence behavior of a stochastic gradient method with adaptive per-coordinate step 
sizes on a non-convex, L-smooth objective with bounded gradient variance. Derive a bound on expected 
gradient norm after T iterations in terms of learning rate schedule, smoothness constant, and variance. 
Identify conditions on the step-size decay under which the bound guarantees convergence to a stationary 
point. Compare the resulting rate against vanilla SGD and explain where the adaptive step size helps or 
hurts asymptotically. Discuss what changes under a non-i.i.d. (e.g. mini-batch correlated) sampling 
assumption. Show full derivations, not just stated results.


PROMPT 5:
Design an attitude determination and control system (ADCS) for a 3U CubeSat in a 500km sun-synchronous 
LEO orbit, required to point a payload camera to within 0.5° pointing accuracy. Specify sensor suite 
(sun sensors, magnetometer, star tracker, gyros) and justify against the accuracy requirement and a 
2W power budget. Size reaction wheels against the spacecraft's moment of inertia and worst-case 
disturbance torques (gravity gradient, aerodynamic drag, solar pressure) at this altitude. Specify a 
desaturation strategy (magnetorquers) given the wheel sizing. Estimate settling time for a 90° slew 
maneuver. Address single-point-of-failure risk in the sensor suite. Show reasoning and calculations, not 
just a component list.


PROMPT 6:
Design the wing structure for a tailless supersonic UAV cruising at Mach 1.8, 15,000m altitude. Select 
and justify a wing planform (delta vs. swept) against supersonic wave drag and low-speed handling during 
launch/recovery. Specify a structural layout (spar/rib arrangement, skin material) that survives peak 
aerodynamic loading at max-Q while minimizing weight. Analyze aeroelastic flutter margin at the design 
speed and identify the governing stiffness constraint. Specify a thermal protection approach for 
aerodynamic heating at sustained supersonic cruise. Address manufacturability via composite layup vs. 
machined aluminum. Show reasoning and calculations, not just a final wing geometry.
